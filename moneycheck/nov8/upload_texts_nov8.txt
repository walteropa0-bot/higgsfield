TITLE
How much money do you need to stop working? 💰

DESCRIPTION
Take your yearly spending and multiply it by 25.
Spend $40,000 a year? Your number is $1,000,000.
Why 25? Because 1 ÷ 25 = 4% – the famous "4% rule".
In the Trinity study, withdrawing 4% in year one and then adjusting for inflation lasted at least 30 years in most
historical cases (US data since 1926, stock/bond portfolios).
It's a rule of thumb, not a guarantee: taxes, fees, your portfolio mix and future returns can change the result.
What's your number? 👇

Sources: William P. Bengen (1994), "Determining Withdrawal Rates Using Historical Data", Journal of Financial Planning.
Cooley, Hubbard & Walz (1998), "Retirement Savings: Choosing a Withdrawal Rate That Is Sustainable", AAII Journal ("Trinity study").
Not financial advice – for educational purposes only.

TITLE (pick one)
Can ONE stock make you rich? Here are the real odds 🎲
1 in 630: the odds of picking a superstar stock

DESCRIPTION
You put $1,000 into one single stock. Best case, it's the next superstar. But how likely is that?
A study tracked every US stock from 1926 to 2025: 29,754 stocks from 29,081 companies.
• Just 46 companies created half of all the $91 trillion in stock market wealth. Picking one of them at random: about 1 in 630.
• Only 3.7% of companies created all of it. That's about 1 in 27.
• Only 48% of stocks had any positive return at all. The typical (median) stock: −6.9%.
• The market as a whole: +1,504,057% – $1 became more than $15,000.
Luck can make you rich. The odds just aren't on your side.
Every dot in the video is one company (29,081 dots).

Sources: Hendrik Bessembinder (2026), "One Hundred Years in the U.S. Stock Markets", working paper, Arizona State University.
Summary: Larry Swedroe, WealthManagement.com, May 26, 2026 – https://www.wealthmanagement.com/investing-strategies/a-century-of-stock-market-winners-and-why-most-stocks-failed-to-deliver
